Lesson 01 · toward fingerstyle

Your First Fingerpicking Pattern

One pattern. Two chords you already know. A steady pulse you can feel.

Today's win: play the p–i–m–a pattern smoothly over Em and C, in time, for one full minute. That's a real fingerstyle skill — and it's the exact cure for the rhythm problem you told me about.

Why this fixes your rhythm

When you strum freely, nothing forces you to stay on the beat — so your hand drifts and you "lose the rhythm." Fingerpicking is different: each finger has one job, in a fixed order. That repeating order becomes a little clock inside your hand. Master a pattern slowly and steadiness comes for free.

New here? Open the Chords & Right-Hand reference in another tab — it explains p·i·m·a and string numbers.

The pattern

Four plucks, one per beat. Hold a chord with your left hand and let the right hand repeat this without stopping:

Beat: 1 2 3 4 Finger: p i m a String: bass 3rd 2nd 1st (then repeat)
  • p (thumb) plays the chord's bass string — for Em the 6th string, for C the 5th string.
  • i = 3rd string · m = 2nd string · a = 1st string. These stay the same for every chord.

The rhythm trainer

Press Start. Each beat lights up a finger and clicks. Don't touch the guitar yet — first just say the letters out loud with the lights: "p… i… m… a." Then pick up the guitar and pluck along. Start slow; only raise the tempo when it feels easy.

Tempo 56BPM
Tip: 56 BPM is a calm walking pace. Loop count: 0
Metronome note: the clicks are generated in your browser (Web Audio) — no download needed. If you hear nothing, click Start once more (browsers require a click before playing sound).

Practice steps (about 15 min)

1Air-pick (2 min). Trainer on at 56 BPM. No guitar. Say "p-i-m-a" with the lights until it's automatic.
2Em only (5 min). Hold Em. Thumb hits the 6th string on beat 1, then i-m-a on 3-2-1. Keep looping. Missed notes are fine — never stop the pulse.
3C only (4 min). Same pattern, but thumb now hits the 5th string. Notice C rings clean when your fingers arch so they don't mute neighbours.
4Switch (4 min). Two loops of Em, two loops of C, repeat. The switch is the hard part — that's exactly the chord-transition skill we're building.

Check yourself

Q. In the pattern p–i–m–a, which finger plays the bass string on beat 1?

Q. You fumble the C chord mid-loop. What should you do?
