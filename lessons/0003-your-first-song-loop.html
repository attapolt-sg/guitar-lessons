Lesson 03 · toward fingerstyle

Your First Song Loop

Four chords, one pattern, endless songs. This is where it starts sounding like music.

Today's win: fingerpick the p-i-m-a pattern through G → D → Em → C, one chord per bar, looping smoothly without stopping. Every note clean (Lesson 02), pulse steady (Lesson 01) — now joined together.

Why these four chords

G, D, Em, and C — in this family — are the backbone of an enormous number of Thai and English pop songs. Learn this one loop and you're a capo away from playing along with hundreds of tracks. It's the most famous progression in popular music for a reason.

Need the shapes? Keep the Chords & Right-Hand reference open.

The loop

One chord per bar. Four plucks per bar (your p-i-m-a from Lesson 01). The thumb plays each chord's bass string:

  • G — thumb on 6th string · D — thumb on 4th string
  • Em — thumb on 6th string · C — thumb on 5th string

The song-loop player

Press Start. It counts four beats per chord, lights each pluck, and — on beat 4 — flashes a red "switch next!" warning so your hand gets ready before the change. That early prep is the secret to not losing the pulse.

Tempo 50BPM
Start at 50 BPM — slower than Lesson 01 on purpose, because switching is hard. Tick "park on one chord" first if you want to warm up the picking before adding changes.

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Name the bass notes (2 min). No playing. Watch the player loop and say each chord's thumb string: "G-six, D-four, Em-six, C-five."
2Two-chord chunks (8 min). Practise just G→D until smooth, then Em→C. Small chunks beat the whole loop.
3Anchor trick (5 min). Between Em and C, your finger 2 barely moves — keep it near the strings as a guide. Look for a common finger between each pair; leave it hovering.
4Full loop (5 min). All four, looping. If you stumble, keep the pulse and rejoin next bar — never stop the beat.
