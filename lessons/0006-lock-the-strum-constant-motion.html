Lesson 06 · rhythm that never wobbles

Lock the Strum — Constant Motion

You said the strum felt steadier than the fingerpick. Good — so let's make it rock solid before we go back to picking.

Today's win: hold one strum pattern around the Em → C → G → D loop for a full minute without losing the beat — using one rule that fixes wobbly rhythm for good.

The one rule: the hand never stops

Here's the mistake almost everyone makes: they move the strumming hand only when they want a strum. Start-stop, start-stop — and the timing drifts, because there's nothing keeping the pulse.

Constant motion. Your strumming hand keeps swinging up and down the whole time, like a pendulum — down on every beat (1 2 3 4), up on every "&" in between. The swing never stops. To play a pattern, you simply let the hand brush the strings on some swings and miss them on others — but the arm keeps moving either way. That non-stop swing is your metronome.

So a "rest" in a pattern is not the hand stopping — it's the hand swinging past the strings without touching them. Keep that in your head and your timing stops drifting almost immediately.

Drill 0 — the mute strum (do this first)

Before you worry about chords, isolate the rhythm alone:

How: lay your fretting hand lightly across all the strings (touching, not pressing) so they don't ring — you'll get a percussive "chk" instead of notes. Now strum the patterns below. With no notes to judge, all you can hear is time — that's the point. Keep the hand swinging.

The player — constant-motion trainer

Pick a pattern. The player sweeps a cursor across all eight swings of the bar: a solid arrow means let it hit, a faded arrow means swing past, no hit. The metronome clicks every beat underneath — that click never changes, so lock to it. Start at 60 BPM.

The three patterns, easiest to hardest

1 · Down-beats only — ↓ · ↓ · ↓ · ↓ ·
One down-strum on each beat. Hand still swings up in between, but misses the strings. The foundation — get this locked first.
2 · All down-up — ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑
Every swing hits: down on the beats, up on the "&"s. Fills the bar with even eighth-notes. Keep down and up equally loud.
3 · Pop pattern — ↓ · ↓ ↑ · ↑ ↓ ↑ ("down, down-up, up-down-up")
The classic. The two misses (after beat 1, and on beat 3) are where the drifting usually starts — so keep the arm swinging through them and trust the constant motion.

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Mute-strum the pulse (4 min). Uncheck "show chord diagrams." Mute the strings with your fretting hand. Play Down-beats only at 60 BPM. Just "chk … chk … chk … chk," dead in time with the click.
2Mute-strum, all down-up (4 min). Same mute, switch to All down-up. Down and up equally strong. Feel the hand pendulum never stop.
3Add the chords, down-beats only (4 min). Re-check chords. Play the Em→C→G→D loop with just down-strums on each beat. Land every chord change on beat 1.
4The pop pattern (5 min). Switch to Pop. Slow the tempo if the misses trip you. Keep the arm swinging through the gaps.
5The one-minute test (3 min). Pick your best pattern and hold it around the full loop for 60 seconds straight without the beat wobbling. That's the win.
