Lesson 07 · fingerstyle, in time at last

The Thumb Keeps the Beat

You locked the strum. Now we move that exact pulse into your picking hand — where the thumb becomes your metronome.

Today's win: fingerpick the Em → C → G → D loop with your thumb landing on every beat — a steady, real fingerstyle groove that keeps the rock-solid time you just built with strumming.

The whole trick in one sentence

Your thumb is the new downstrum. In Lesson 06 your strumming arm swung down on every beat and that kept time. Now the thumb (p) takes that job: it plucks a bass note on every single beat — 1, 2, 3, 4 — like a heartbeat. The fingers (i · m) just fill the gaps in between. The pulse doesn't change; only the hand doing it changes.

Because your thumb never skips a beat, the timing can't drift — the same reason "constant motion" fixed your strumming. This is why we drilled rhythm first: fingerstyle needs a steady thumb, and now you have one.

The pattern

Eight plucks per bar, over "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &". The thumb (p) hits on every number; a finger hits on every "&":

Pattern: (p+a) m p i p m p i
Beat 1 is a pinch — thumb and ring finger (a) pluck together, so the downbeat lands with a little accent, just like the strong first strum in your pop pattern. After that: thumb on 2, 3, 4; and m · i alternate on the "&"s.
  • p (thumb) plays the chord's bass string: Em → 6th, C → 5th, G → 6th, D → 4th. The player names it under the diagram.
  • i · m · a pluck strings 3 · 2 · 1 (G-B-high E) — the same fingers-to-strings map as your very first pattern.
  • Say "beat" out loud on every thumb pluck. If you can hear a steady thumb, you've won.

The player — feel the pulse carry over

Start in Pop strum mode (the one you locked) to set the pulse, then switch to Thumb pulse over the same click — notice the thumb lands exactly where your downstrums did. Start at 56 BPM.

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Set the pulse with the strum (3 min). Pop-strum mode at 56 BPM. Get the loop feeling locked, like last lesson.
2Thumb alone, no fingers (5 min). Switch to Thumb pulse, but only play the thumb on every beat — ignore the i·m for now. Just p p p p, one bass note per beat, dead in time.
3Add the fingers on the "&"s (7 min). Now let m · i fill the gaps: (p+a) m p i p m p i. Keep the thumb steady — the fingers fit around it, they don't disturb it.
4The one-minute test (5 min). Hold the full pattern around Em→C→G→D for 60 seconds. Success = the thumb never stutters at a chord change.
