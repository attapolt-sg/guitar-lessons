Lesson 09 · your first full song

Stand By Me — Your First Full Song

Back to strumming — and this time we play a whole song, start to finish, with the four chords you already own: G, Em, C, D.

Today's win: strum all the way through the Stand By Me loop with a steady, non-stop pattern — landing every chord change right on beat 1, without the rhythm wobbling.

Why this song

"Stand By Me" (Ben E. King) rides one four-chord progression — the famous "50s progression" — around and around for the entire song. Nothing new for your fretting hand: it's G, Em, C, D, all shapes from your reference sheet. So every bit of your attention goes to the strum and the chord changes in time — exactly the skill you locked in Lesson 06.

The fast part is bars 5–6. C and D each last only one bar, so the C → D change comes quickly — one bar of C, then straight to D. That's the one spot to slow down and drill first.

The strum — keep the hand swinging

Use the constant-motion rule from Lesson 06: the strumming hand never stops — down on every beat, up on every "&" — and you just let it hit the strings on some swings and miss on others. Start with down-beats only, then graduate to the pop pattern once the changes feel easy.

Making the changes clean

G → Em (bar 2 → 3). You have two whole bars of G, so there's no rush. Right before beat 1 of bar 3, lift the G shape and drop into Em — it's one of the easiest shapes, so aim to land it exactly on the downbeat.
C → D (bar 5 → 6). The quick one. Both are one bar only. Practice just these two bars in a small loop first: C (4 beats) → D (4 beats), slowly, until the switch stops feeling rushed.
D → G (bar 6 → 7). This turns the loop back to the top. Keep your strumming hand swinging through the change so the pulse never pauses while your fretting hand moves.

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Say the map (2 min). Count the bars out loud with the strip above: "G, G, Em, Em, C, D, G, G." Know where you are before adding hands.
2Down-beats only, whole loop (5 min). One down-strum per beat at 60 BPM. Just land each chord change on beat 1. Don't worry about a fancy strum yet.
3Drill the fast switch (4 min). Loop only C → D (bars 5–6) on its own until it's smooth.
4All down-up (4 min). Fill the bar with even down-up strums around the full loop. Keep down and up equally loud.
5The pop pattern (5 min). Switch to Pop. Slow the tempo if the misses trip you, then nudge it back up. Play the whole song loop three times without stopping.

Tell me the result

Message me: (1) could you get all the way around the 8-bar loop without stopping, and at what BPM, (2) which strum pattern you reached (down-beats / all down-up / pop), and (3) was the quick C → D switch the hard part, or something else? Once this feels solid, Lesson 10 can add the little bass-note lead-ins that make it sound like the record. 🎶
