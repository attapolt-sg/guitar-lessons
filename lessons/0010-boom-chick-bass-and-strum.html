Lesson 10 · make it sound like the record

Boom-Chick — Add a Bass Note to Your Strum

Same song, same four chords — but now the thumb plays a bass note on the strong beats and you strum in between. Instantly fuller, more "produced" sound.

Today's win: play the Stand By Me loop with a boom-chick feel — a bass note on beats 1 & 3, a strum on beats 2 & 4 — all the way around, staying in time.

What "boom-chick" means

You already have two skills: the steady strum (Lesson 06/09) and the thumb bass pulse (Lesson 07). Boom-chick just splits them across the beat:

Boom = a single bass note (the chord's root, played by your thumb) on beats 1 and 3.
Chick = a strum of the upper strings on beats 2 and 4.
Say it out loud: "boom-chick-boom-chick." That alternation is the engine of countless folk, country, and pop songs.

Which bass note for each chord

The "boom" is the root of the chord — the note the chord is named after. Here's the string to aim your thumb at for each chord in the song:

ChordBass note (root)String to pluck
GG6th string (low E), 3rd fret
EmE6th string (low E), open
CC5th string (A), 3rd fret
DD4th string (D), open
Good news: for G and Em the bass note is the lowest string (6th), which you're already fretting as part of the chord. For C aim one string in (5th), and for D aim at the open 4th string. Your fingers are already there — you're just picking out one string first.

The two patterns

1 · Boom-chick (basic) — B · ↓ · B · ↓
Bass on beats 1 and 3, one down-strum on beats 2 and 4. The pure alternation — get this locked first.
2 · Boom-chick-a (fuller) — B · ↓ ↑ · B · ↓ ↑
Same bass notes, but each strum becomes a down-up. Adds a gentle bounce without changing the "boom" feel.

Level-up: a bass walk-up into G

To sound even more like the record, connect the turnaround (bar 6 → 7, D → G) with a little walk-up on the bass strings instead of a plain strum on beat 4 of the D bar:

On the last beat of the D bar, walk the bass up E (open 6th) → F♯ (2nd fret, 6th) → G (3rd fret, 6th) so you land on the G bass note right on beat 1. Try it slowly, by itself, a few times before adding it to the loop. (It's optional — nail the plain boom-chick first.)

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Find the four bass notes (3 min). Hold each chord and pluck just its bass string from the table above. Say the note name as you play it: "G… E… C… D."
2Boom-chick on one chord (4 min). Stay on G. Bass (beat 1), strum (beat 2), bass (beat 3), strum (beat 4). Slow, even, at 60 BPM.
3Whole loop, basic pattern (6 min). Play the full 8-bar loop with boom-chick. Let the bass note land exactly on the chord change.
4Fuller pattern (4 min). Switch to boom-chick-a. Keep the booms solid; the added up-strums shouldn't rush the beat.
5Optional walk-up (3 min). Add the D→G bass walk-up on the turnaround. Loop the whole song twice with it.
