Lesson 11 · real fingerstyle at last

Travis Picking — Alternating Bass

The rolling fingerstyle sound. Your thumb keeps a steady alternating bass while your fingers pick a gentle pattern on top — the two hands finally fully independent.

Today's win: keep your thumb alternating between two bass strings on every beat while your fingers pick the top strings in between — a continuous, rolling Travis pattern, on the same G / Em / C / D chords you already own.

What Travis picking is

In Lesson 10 your thumb played a bass note and your hand strummed the chord. Travis picking replaces the strum with individual finger plucks — and asks the thumb to keep moving between two bass strings instead of repeating one.

The engine: the thumb plays a bass note on every beat, bouncing between a low string and a higher string (low–high–low–high). On top of that steady pulse, your fingers (i, m, a) pluck the treble strings on the "&"s between the beats. The two layers interlock into one rolling sound — that's the whole trick.

The idea that makes it possible

The thumb is on autopilot. Just like the metronome pulse from Lesson 07, the thumb's job never changes — low, high, low, high — so you can stop thinking about it. Once the thumb runs on its own, your fingers are free to add the pattern on top. Get the thumb steady first, alone, before adding a single finger.

Which two bass strings for each chord

The thumb alternates between the chord's root (its low bass note) and a higher string inside the same shape. You're already holding every one of these — the thumb just picks them out one at a time:

ChordBeat 1 & 3 (low)Beat 2 & 4 (high)
G6th string (G, 3rd fret)4th string (D, open)
Em6th string (E, open)4th string (E, 2nd fret)
C5th string (C, 3rd fret)4th string (D, open)
D4th string (D, open)3rd string (A, 2nd fret)
Note on C: its high alternate here is the open 4th string (D). That's a passing note, not strictly inside the C chord — but it flies by so fast it sounds perfectly natural, and it's the easiest reach. It's the standard folk choice.

The two patterns

1 · Thumb only — p · p · p · p (low, high, low, high)
Just the alternating bass, one thumb pluck per beat. This is your foundation — it must be dead steady before you add fingers. Think of it as Lesson 10's "boom" happening on every beat.
2 · Full Travis roll — thumb on the beats, a finger on each "&"
The thumb keeps its low–high–low–high pulse, and your fingers pick a top string on every "&". The result is the continuous rolling pattern. Keep the thumb loud and even; let the finger notes float lightly on top.

Making it click

Thumb independence is the whole lesson. If the fingers make your thumb hesitate, stop, drop back to thumb only for a minute, then re-add just one finger note at a time.
Pinch the downbeat. On beat 1, the thumb bass and a finger note often land together (a pinch, like Lesson 08). Landing those two exactly together is what makes the pattern lock.

Practice routine (about 20 min)

1Thumb alone, one chord (5 min). Hold G. Alternate the thumb: 6th, 4th, 6th, 4th — low, high, low, high — at 52 BPM. Eyes closed, until it needs no thought.
2Thumb around the loop (4 min). Keep the alternating thumb going while changing chords G → Em → C → D. Use the bass table above for each chord's two strings.
3Add one finger (5 min). Switch the player to the full roll. On one chord, add just the finger notes on the "&"s. Keep the thumb from flinching.
4Full roll, one chord (4 min). Play the complete Travis roll on G until it feels smooth and even.
5Full roll around the loop (2 min). Try the whole G → Em → C → D loop with the roll, slowly, once through. That's the win.
